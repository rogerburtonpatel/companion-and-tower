\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Coinductive techniques\ldots{} Such techniques exist in proof assistants at two
levels: foundational and abstracted. Foundational is paco/gpaco~\cite{paco,gpaco},
enhanced coinduction~\cite{pous-companion,schafer-smolka}; abstracted is
itrees~\cite{itrees} and ctrees~\cite{ctrees}.

Larger developments are often built off of abstraction, but sometimes use
low-level reasoning (Vellvm~\cite{vellvm} using paco proofs).

We rewrote itrees from paco to enhanced coinduction, expecting large
improvements.

Some improvements, but disappointing.

So we rewrote the enhanced coinduction library to upstream a lot of desired
behavior that was duplicated in itrees.

With this, we saw a \gap{\%} improvement.
\note{Pick which number carries the headline here. Candidates, all measured:
tactic characters in \code{theories/} $-9.4\%$ against the paco baseline (and
$-56\%$ against the first port); proof characters $-8.4\%$ in \code{theories/},
$-11.8\%$ overall; build time recovering from $+25.6\%$ to $+4.1\%$.}

The documentation of this study, and the new enhanced coinduction and itrees
libraries, are our primary contribution.
