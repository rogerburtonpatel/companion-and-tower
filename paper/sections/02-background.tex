\subsection{The Need for Observational Equality}

Idea 1: If all the observations $\tau$ or $\beta$ within the process align, the
two processes are (observationally) equal.

Idea 2: This can work even when the processes are \emph{infinite}.

Two prominent examples of non-well-founded objects: Label Transition Systems
(LTSs), often defined with \code{Prop}; and Infinite Objects (Processes,
Streams\ldots), defined with \code{CoInductive}, \code{CoFixpoint}. Infinite
objects are the focus of this paper.

\emph{``Coinduction is used for writing an infinite proof about infinite
objects''}

Our core case study is coinduction for bisimulation, an instance of this
framework. But unary coinductive predicates are very common: think properties
for single programs!

Goal: need to be able to show objects ``observe'' the same \emph{forever}. But
we can't do this manually\ldots

\subsection{Theoretical Background}

Coinduction in general admits models in terms of final coalgebras. But also
order- and lattice-theoretic ones in terms of greatest fix points. This latter
is simpler and sufficient for defining relations, and is the focus of this
paper.

\subsection{Lattices}

A lattice is a set, a preorder defined on elements of the set, and supremum
(least upper bound) and infimum (greatest lower bound) operators. We write this
as $\langle X, \leqslant, \bigvee \rangle$. (Greatest lower bounds can be
derived from least upper bounds) w.l.o.g.

A \emph{complete} lattice is a lattice in which \textbf{any subset of $X$ admits
a least upper bound and greatest lower bound.} A natural consequence of this is
that every complete lattice has a maximum element (top, $\top$) and minimum
element (bottom, $\bot$).

A function on a lattice is \emph{monotone} if it respects the preorder. That is,
\emph{mon f} means $\forall x\, y,\ x \leqslant y \to f\,x \leqslant f\,y$. A
\emph{fix point} of a function $f$ is an element $x$ s.t. $f\,x = x$.
\textbf{Every monotone function on a complete lattice admits a complete lattice
of its fix points (Knaster--Tarski).} This means every monotone function has a
\emph{greatest fix point} \textcolor{blue}{-- this will be important}.

\subsection{Focus: Lattices of Relations}

These lattices exist because: (1) if \code{X} is a complete lattice, then
\code{A -> X} is a complete lattice; (2) \code{Prop} is a complete lattice, with
\code{<=} $\triangleq$ \code{->}.

\code{<=} on lattices of relations is determined \emph{pointwise:} \code{R <= S}
when \code{forall x, R x <= S x}.

Our study: lattice of relations on states, \code{state -> state -> Prop}.

\subsection{Why is this important for proving observational equivalence?}

Consider a monotone function \code{b : mon (state -> state -> Prop)}. \code{b}
describes \textcolor{blue}{`being observed and taking a step.'} In particular,
\code{(b p q R)} means \code{p} and \code{q} take the same step together, and
are then related by \code{R}.

Consider a fix point of \code{b}: \code{(fp b) = b (fp b)}. If we relate two
systems by \code{(fp b)}, they take the same \code{b} steps together, forever.

Fortunately\ldots we know \code{(fp b)} exists, for any monotone \code{b}. We
know (at least two) such relations exist: the \textbf{least} and \textbf{greatest}
fix points of any monotone function on our system. Our systems may run forever,
so we pick the \underline{greatest} fix point (\code{gfp}).

Then \code{gfp b} is bisimilarity, which we will take to be observational
equivalence.

\subsection{The game: we want to find the greatest fix point of \code{b}}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item As a fix point, it unfolds infinitely:
        \code{gfp b = b (gfp b) = b (b (gfp b)) = ...}
  \item As the greatest fix point, it is inhabited for infinite judgements,
        while the least fix point is not.
\end{enumerate}

There are various ways to construct \code{gfp b} from \code{b}. Perhaps the most
famous is a corollary of \textbf{Knaster-Tarski}: the lattice of fixpoints of
\code{b} is complete, and its top is \code{gfp b}. Then the proof method to show
membership in \code{gfp b} is to show membership of anything under it.

\textbf{Knaster Tarski:} Game is to find R that satisfies this diagram. Or this
definition: \code{R <= bR} (R is a post-fix point of \code{b}). Then you win:
\code{R a b -> gfp b a b}.

x \leqslant \nu b$, is the one the rest of the paper uses.}
