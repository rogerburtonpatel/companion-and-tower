\subsection{Initial Results are good}

\textbf{Up-to proofs got shorter.}

Broadly, about a 10-12\% decrease in proof and definition verbosity.

Best recorded improvement: $1{,}490$ words $9{,}725$ characters $\to$ $570$ words
$4{,}391$ characters. \gap{The side-by-side from slide 67, paco $407$ words
$2{,}610$ characters against tower $258$ words $1{,}364$ characters.}

\note{\textbf{``proof and definition verbosity'' is half right.} Proof fell
$8.7\%$ in \code{theories/} and $12.0\%$ across the whole development at this
checkpoint, so 10--12\% is fair for proof. Definitions did not move: $-0.2\%$ and
$+0.3\%$. \Cref{tab:headline} has both. Either drop ``and definition'' or say
proof fell 10--12\% while definitions stayed flat, which is a cleaner setup for
\Cref{sec:problems} because it isolates where the cost went.}

\subsection{Initial Results, pt. 2}

\textbf{Many proofs by coinduction got shorter.} $209$ words $1{,}281$ characters
$\to$ $117$ words $671$ characters.

\textbf{They also use fewer library tactics:} This example: 7 vs 2.

\subsection{Other informal observations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Proofs by coinduction and up-to proofs look very similar
  \item We depend on a smaller library that builds 5x faster
  \item Proofs are easier for me to read and explain
  \item LOC \# is theoretically* much lower
  \item No need to remember arities, everything is \code{gfp}
\end{itemize}

\note{Two of these are unmeasured and a reviewer will ask. ``builds 5x faster''
is not in the released data, and ``LOC \# is theoretically much lower'' has an
asterisk in the talk with no footnote. Either measure them or mark them
explicitly as informal, which the section heading already does.}
